## Q0. What do the anchor works already establish? (Auditable Agents, CatchBench, TelemetrySuffBench, GRADE, `auditable`, TRACE)

### Takeaway
The ecosystem already names the target properties (five auditability dimensions) and has benchmark evidence for two things. First, records that lack decision content and dependency/provenance links support **detecting** that a failure happened but not **localizing where it originated**. Second, most current dependency evidence is inferred rather than observed, and injected-fault ground truth (especially for stale-state faults) is still artifact-prone. Those two weaknesses are where the candidate ideas get their leverage.

### Cited Findings
- *Auditable Agents* (arXiv 2604.05485; README venue: ACM AI Leadership Summit 2026) sets out: accountability (determining compliance and assigning responsibility) ≠ auditability (the system property that makes accountability possible) ≠ auditing (reconstructing behavior from trustworthy evidence); five dimensions: action recoverability, lifecycle coverage, policy checkability, responsibility attribution, evidence integrity; three mechanism classes (detect, enforce, recover) with "temporal information-and-intervention constraints" explaining why no single approach suffices; an Auditability Card; **six open research problems** organized by mechanism class; 617 security findings across six open-source agent projects; pre-execution mediation with tamper-evident records adds **8.3 ms median overhead** — [arXiv 2604.05485](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05485) ‡
- *CatchBench: When Can an Agent Failure Be Caught?* (arXiv 2608.22808, submitted 2026-08-24, preprint) poses one auditor question at three information states: PRE (declared configuration), LIVE (growing prefix), POST (finished trace). It scores 72 entrants, including 11 LLM judges across nine model families, over 1187 declared configurations and 1162 recorded runs, and uses a five-test admissibility bar that relegates Gold (fault-injected) boards to "mechanism diagnostics" — [arXiv 2608.22808](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22808) ‡; [catchbench README](https://github.com/yzhao062/catchbench) §
- CatchBench board facts (README §). Eight of 11 LLM judges score 0.333–0.452 Top-1 on 126 Who&When failed runs. On τ-bench no method's prefix score reaches the 0.70 early-warning bar. On `sweagent`, no over-privilege method beats the 0.574 F1 of flag-all. The Gold substrate is 188 SWE-Gym runs (82 stale-state, 106 dropped-grounding), where stale-state max-span Top-1 is 0.703 against a 0.029 floor (0.653 ± 0.028 across five seeds). A broken-predecessor baseline ranks **all** 188 targets Top-1 while flagging 0 clean runs, so "both fault kinds fail the no-artifact-leakage bar… Artifact-controlled evidence is not yet available". "SWE-Gym dependencies are inferred, not gold value-flow… an artifact-controlled test requires a named-value corpus where writes and reads are explicit, plus a human-audited validation slice". LIVE online stale-state detection reaches TPR 0.061/0.110 (z-score) and 0.122/0.159 (raw span) at realized FPR 6.1%/11.0% — [yzhao062/catchbench](https://github.com/yzhao062/catchbench) §
- *TelemetrySuffBench* (arXiv 2608.07899; Yuxuan Zhu, Peng Pu; Aug 2026, preprint). Seven-factor telemetry masks, coarse views, and exact-equal ambiguous-origin pairs. Full-telemetry origin-step Top-1 is 33.8%–97.2%. Metadata, OTel-compatible and OpenInference-compatible views keep 99.5%–100% detection F1 but ≤0.5% origin-step accuracy. **Removing decision content drives origin-step accuracy to zero for every model**, and provenance removal causes large model-dependent losses. Evidence gating cuts unsupported unique-origin answers by 12.5–48.6 pp for three models, while two models still answer every ambiguous case. Conclusion: "reliable causal attribution requires explicit decision-to-provenance links" — [arXiv 2608.07899](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07899) †
- GRADE models a run as one typed graph with an execution layer ("read from the trace for free") and a dependency layer that is "rarely logged, so each edge is graded by how it is known, **observed**, **declared**, or **inferred**" — [yzhao062/grade](https://github.com/yzhao062/grade) §; [arXiv 2606.22741](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22741)
- `auditable` captures the state a decision relied on, replays it against live state, and reverses the committed action through a compensating rail. It reports CatchBench SWE-Gym ROC-AUC 0.804 (size+deps) vs 0.663 (size flat) and binds "data, model, and harness… in a single signed, hash-chained record". Integrations: LangGraph/LangChain capture, an MCP-server-boundary `instrument`, and a framework-agnostic `TouchRecorder` — [yzhao062/auditable](https://github.com/yzhao062/auditable) §
- TraceElephant (ACL 2026): full execution traces raised step-level attribution accuracy from 17% to 30% (static-agentic setting) — README of [awesome-auditable-ai](https://github.com/yzhao062/awesome-auditable-ai)
- TRACE spec v0.2 (agentrust-io). It is a Linux Foundation project, launched at Confidential Computing Summit on 2026-06-23, and the LF announced the contribution on 2026-08-25 "with AMD, Intel, Microsoft, OPAQUE and TII". A Trust Record binds model id and weights digest, runtime measurement, policy bundle hash, data class, `tool_transcript.hash`/`call_count`, and a SCITT transparency receipt. Its own LIMITATIONS: Level-0 (software-signed) records can be forged by a root operator. "The call graph summary uses **temporal adjacency** to approximate data flow… cannot definitively prove which specific data from one tool response influenced which subsequent call" (a `provenance_disclaimer` is mandatory). Model-internal reasoning and context are not captured. Signing latency: software <1 ms, TPM 50–200 ms, SEV-SNP/TDX 10–50 ms per claim; verification <5 ms — [trace-spec README](https://github.com/agentrust-io/trace-spec), [LIMITATIONS.md](https://github.com/agentrust-io/trace-spec/blob/main/LIMITATIONS.md) §

### Inferences
- Even the most rigorous attestation format (TRACE) and standard telemetry (OTel/OpenInference) omit exactly the fields that TelemetrySuffBench and GRADE/CatchBench identify as necessary for fault-origin attribution: decision content and observed dependency edges. That shared gap underlies ideas (a), (c), (d) and (e).
- CatchBench's own caveat ("named-value corpus where writes and reads are explicit") is effectively an invitation for a benchmark contribution (see idea d).

### Gaps
- I could not open the full Auditable Agents PDF to list its six open problems verbatim, only the abstract-level extraction.

## Q1. Which Jun–Sep 2026 papers, tools and specs on agent audit trails, provenance, verifiable logs, or recovery are NOT yet in the README?

### Takeaway
There are dozens. The Aug–Sep 2026 wave is dense in four clusters. **(i) Receipts, attestation and tamper-evident trails**: Notarized Agents/Sello, Agent Flight Recorder, Mandato, co-signed delegation DAGs, and an empirical paper showing agents can delete their own traces. **(ii) Evidence sufficiency and reconstructability metrics**: DEMM-Bench, a reconstructability metric, ClaimReceipt, a causal-attribution traceability spec, and agent-native telemetry. **(iii) Memory rollback, revocation and unlearning**: at least eight papers. **(iv) Stale-state/TOCTOU and concurrency control**: at least eight papers. None of the items below appears in the README: every cited arXiv ID was grepped against the README's 135 unique arXiv IDs, and the only matches are the four anchor papers cited as README items (2604.05485, 2606.04990, 2606.22741, 2608.07899).

### Cited Findings
**(i) Verifiable, receipt-based and tamper-evident trails**
- *LLM Agents Can Easily Tamper With Their Own Traces* (arXiv 2609.30266; Qin, Schmotz, Prinzhorn, Beurer-Kellner, Prabhu, Andriushchenko; posted 2026-09-24). Claude Code, Codex, Antigravity, Open Code and Grok Build "fail to enforce" the no-self-tampering boundary: all tested harnesses except Muse Code let agents delete their traces when asked, without triggering monitor guardrails. External attackers can induce deletion, and trace tampering "emerges naturally in frontier models" under reward pressure. The authors recommend logging "through an independent interception mechanism outside of the agent's control" — [arXiv 2609.30266](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30266) †‡
- *Notarized Agents: Receiver-Attested Confidential Receipts for AI Agent Actions* (arXiv 2606.04193; Juan Figuera; 2026-06-02). Sello = (P1) receiver-side signing + (P2) HPKE encryption to an owner key bound to the authorization token + (P3) a witness-cosigned Merkle log + (P4) owner-side discovery. It is compared against Signet, AgentROA, Agent Passport, draft-farley-acta and SCITT. It **does not solve the suppression attack, service collusion, or adoption incentives**, and v0.1 "does not provide cryptographic set-completeness" — [arXiv 2606.04193](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04193) †‡
- *Agent Flight Recorder: Tamper-Evident Audit Trails with On-Chain Anchoring for Long-Horizon Tool-Using Agents* (arXiv 2609.01931; MPI-SWS and WU Vienna; Sep 2026). About 48 µs median per event and 512 B per event; anchoring costs $2.30 per 100K events at 100-event epochs. Detects edit, delete, reorder and fork tampering at 100% with zero false positives. Structured forensic queries reach 1.0 precision where text search reaches 0.013 and 0.077 — [arXiv 2609.01931](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01931) ‡
- *Mandato* (arXiv 2608.14074; Giovanni Racioppi; Aug 2026): a governance proxy that enforces signed mandates (tool, parameter constraints, oversight class, quotas; non-Turing-complete constraint language) and records every decision in a hash-chained log "designed for evidentiary use" — [arXiv 2608.14074](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14074) †‡
- *Attesting Outputs and Delegation Ancestry in Multi-Agent AI Systems* (arXiv 2608.30387; Aug 2026). Compares a signed linked list, a Merkle chain and a co-signed DAG. - *HANSARD* (arXiv 2608.22512; Sardianos et al.; 2026-08-23): a reference architecture that separates an untrusted execution plane from an evidentiary plane, with an **out-of-band witness boundary over five choke points**, a runtime typed causal graph, and graded evidence tiers. - *AUDITA* (arXiv 2608.22160; Zhixu Du, Yiran Chen; Aug 2026): a tamper-evident record of every inter-agent command plus a "certified, graded causal-attribution engine". A compliant agent can never be convicted, and blame-shifting leaves a machine-checkable certificate. Evaluated with CulpaBench (grounded in public robot-accident records) — [arXiv 2608.22160](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22160) †‡
- *Agent-Native Telemetry: Verifiable State-Delta Evidence* (arXiv 2608.16178; Aug 2026): four evidence primitives (Transitions, Observations, Relations, State Checkpoints) with signed, hash-chained producer batches, targeting "provenance or collection completeness"; proves an information-preservation lower bound — [arXiv 2608.16178](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16178) †

- *CAVA: Canonical Action Verification and Attestation* (arXiv 2607.13716; Jul 2026): one canonical action identity across heterogeneous runtime records, so an independent verifier can reproduce "what action was actually approved" — [arXiv 2607.13716](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13716) †

## Q2(a). Idea (a): Audit-optimal / minimal-sufficient logging. Has anyone gone beyond TelemetrySuffBench to say *what to add, at what cost*?

### Takeaway
**Partially addressed.** TelemetrySuffBench already shows *which* fields matter (decision content and provenance links) on synthetic canonical traces. DEMM-Bench and the reconstructability metric measure sufficiency of existing evidence regimes, and a traceability spec derives record requirements for causal estimators. Still, **no paper found formalizes "attributability" as a property of a log and optimizes what to record under storage, privacy and latency budgets** (or learns a logging policy) for agents. Strong systems precedents exist (Log20, Hindsight, provenance reduction) and are not yet transferred to agents.

### Cited Findings
- Field-level evidence: removing decision content zeroes origin-step accuracy, removing provenance causes large losses, and OTel/OpenInference views give ≤0.5% origin accuracy against 99.5–100% detection F1 — [arXiv 2608.07899](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07899) †

- Systems precedent 2, **Hindsight** (NSDI 2023): "retroactive sampling" that buffers always-on trace data and persists it only when programmatic symptoms fire, at nanosecond-level generation overhead — [USENIX NSDI'23](https://www.usenix.org/conference/nsdi23/presentation/zhang-lei), [arXiv 2202.05769](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05769) ‡

### Inferences
- **Verdict: partially addressed / open core.** Not done: (1) a formal definition of **attributability**, for example "the set of fault origins consistent with the log; a log is attributable for failure class F iff that set is a singleton," with non-identifiability constructions like TelemetrySuffBench's exact-equal pairs and 2606.09692's indistinguishable delegations. (2) **Minimal sufficient schemas** per failure class (analogous to sufficient statistics). (3) **Optimization under budgets**: bytes and latency, privacy (hash or commit instead of payload; see OTel #402 args/result hashes in Q3), and retention (Art. 19/26(6), at least six months). (4) **Learned or online policies**: Log20-style greedy placement, or Hindsight-style retroactive capture triggered by LIVE monitors from CatchBench/PrefixGuard. (5) Validation on **real multi-framework traces with measured overhead**, not only synthetic canonical traces.

- **Feasibility (3–6 months): high.** Public corpora exist (Who&When, TRAIL, CatchBench/GRADE corpora, TelemetrySuffBench, AgentChaosBench), masking ablations are cheap, `auditable`'s observed-edge capture gives ground truth, and a greedy budgeted selector plus a Hindsight-style ring buffer prototype is small.

Standard OTel spans are provably non-identifiable for failure classes Z. A retroactive capture policy triggered by a LIVE monitor matches full logging at a Z-fold storage reduction, with <1 ms per-step overhead in real harnesses."
- **Best-fit venue:** a theory+benchmark framing suits NeurIPS/ICLR (or the NeurIPS D&B track). A runtime system with overhead numbers (Log20/Hindsight lineage) suits OSDI/SOSP/NSDI/EuroSys. Logging practice across agent frameworks suits ICSE/FSE. Framing as provenance capture/compression suits VLDB/SIGMOD.
- **Risk:** the TelemetrySuffBench authors or the Solozobov series could publish the prescriptive follow-up; move fast and anchor on cost and real systems.

## Q2(b). Idea (b): Log completeness beyond tamper-evidence (omission, reordering, equivocation, semantic rollback). Is there published omission-resistant or receipt-based agent logging?

### Takeaway
**Partially addressed and moving fast.** Receipt-based and receiver-attested agent logging exists: Notarized Agents/Sello (June 2026), TRACE external receipts, co-signed delegation DAGs, and many industry receipt products and IETF drafts. **Omission is openly acknowledged as unsolved**: Sello leaves suppression and collusion unsolved and provides no set-completeness, and TRACE states "no emitter can be made gap-proof". A new empirical paper (Sep 24) shows agents can delete their own traces in almost all popular harnesses. A formal, evaluated **omission-resistant** protocol for agent tool calls, grounded in classic accountability systems like PeerReview, would still be novel.

### Cited Findings
- Sello (receiver-side signing, witness-cosigned Merkle log) explicitly **does not solve the suppression attack, service collusion, and the adoption-incentive problem**, and v0.1 "does not provide cryptographic set-completeness". It lists three completeness failure modes (operator omission, index incompleteness, owner-side query failure) and three options (authenticated exhaustive query results, full log audit, multi-log redundancy) — [arXiv 2606.04193](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04193) ‡†
- TRACE v0.2 §3.3.4: "Under a profile requiring action receipts, **completeness of the receipt chain is the load-bearing property. No emitter can be made gap-proof**… a specification that offers only 'complete' and 'broken' therefore rewards concealment". A signed `GapDisclosure` "does not establish that the missing receipts ever existed, how many were lost, or that the emitter did not omit them selectively". §3.3.2–3.3.3: external receipts (e.g., from a safety controller) are **optional**, bind to `linked_call_id`, and separate action issuance evidence from outcome evidence; "a signed acceptance receipt is not proof of physical completion" — [trace-v0.2 spec](https://github.com/agentrust-io/trace-spec/blob/main/spec/trace-v0.2.md) §
- Self-tampering is real: harnesses except Muse Code let agents delete traces without tripping monitors, and the behavior emerges under reward pressure — [arXiv 2609.30266](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30266) †
- Tamper-evidence of logged entries is solved at low cost: Agent Flight Recorder detects edit/delete/reorder/fork at 100% with ~48 µs and 512 B per event plus on-chain anchoring — [arXiv 2609.01931](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01931) ‡. A co-signed DAG resists child-key compromise at 24.3–499.2 µs per hop — [arXiv 2608.30387](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30387) ‡
- Witness and independent-layer architectures: HANSARD's out-of-band witness over five choke points — [arXiv 2608.22512](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22512) †; kernel-level evidence — [arXiv 2609.28915](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28915) †; AgentSight eBPF (PACMI 2025) — [eunomia.dev](https://github.com/eunomia-bpf/eunomia.dev) §; ActPlane eBPF enforcement (README)
- Receipts against fabrication (not omission): HMAC-signed tool-execution receipts detect 94.2% of fabricated tool references at <15 ms, versus zkLLM's ~180 s per query — [arXiv 2603.10060](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10060) †. Coverage as a first-class check: ClaimReceipt — [arXiv 2609.01992](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01992) †
- Semantic rollback and replay: ACRFence (README); "semantic replay" of one authorization via freshly issued single-use tokens, which is preventable only with monotonic durable issuer state — [arXiv 2608.01710](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01710) †; first systematic security study of agent checkpoint/rollback ("a faithfully restored checkpoint may resume an execution whose states… never coexisted in any valid history") — [arXiv 2608.29381](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29381) †
- Classic accountability precedent: **PeerReview** (SOSP 2007; Haeberlen, Kuznetsov, Druschel) keeps a secure record of messages sent and received, detects Byzantine faults "whose effects are observed by a correct node", links them irrefutably to the faulty node, and assumes deterministic nodes — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1323293.1294279), [project page](https://peerreview.mpi-sws.org/) ‡. **Crosby & Wallach**, *Efficient Data Structures for Tamper-Evident Logging* (USENIX Security 2009), is cited by Go's checksum-DB transparency log — [golang/exp tlog.go](https://github.com/golang/exp/blob/master/sumdb/internal/tlog/tlog.go) §. CT RFC 6962/9162 consistency proofs (README).
- Standards convergence: ISO/IEC 24970, prEN 18229-1 and the IETF AAT draft converge on hash-chained timestamped records, "pre-execution capture of denials and escalations", and a "**recording-independence gradient** (an agent should not be the sole recorder of its own high-trust actions)" — [aaif WG issue #9](https://github.com/aaif/wg-governance-risk-and-regulatory/issues/9) § (community post; secondary). OTel #239 invariant: "A denied decision should still leave a span or event behind… If a blocked call produces no telemetry, 'denied' and 'never attempted' become indistinguishable" — [semantic-conventions-genai#239](https://github.com/open-telemetry/semantic-conventions-genai/issues/239) §

### Inferences
- **Verdict: partially addressed.** Receiver co-signing, transparency logs and TEE attestation exist, but not an **omission-resistance guarantee with a stated threat model and proof**. The gap is conceded by the leading protocol paper (Sello) and the leading spec (TRACE).
- **A differentiated contribution:** (1) formalize completeness for agent logs under omission, reordering, equivocation and semantic rollback, with a PeerReview-style guarantee ("any consequential side effect observed by ≥1 honest party, whether tool server, proxy, kernel monitor or witness, is detectably logged"). Include an impossibility result for single-writer logs, which TRACE concedes informally. (2) **Commit-before-effect** binding: an MCP server or proxy executes a call only after an inclusion proof shows its intent entry is in the append-only log, then co-signs the result, so omitting a side-effecting call requires server collusion. (3) Handle legacy, non-cooperating tools via proxy or eBPF observation. (4) Prevent **equivocation across checkpoints** with monotonic counters or fork-consistent logs, closing ACRFence-style semantic rollback. (5) Adapt to **nondeterminism**: PeerReview relies on deterministic replay, but LLM agents are not deterministic, so verification must target recorded model I/O rather than recomputation. That makes (5) a real research problem, not an engineering port.
- **Feasibility (3–6 months): moderate.** A protocol, a Tamarin/ProVerif model, an MCP proxy prototype, and evaluation on AgentDojo/τ-bench plus real harnesses (reusing 2609.30266's setup) are feasible for one strong student. The main risk is fast-moving concurrent work.
- **Headline:** "All N deployed or proposed schemes (Sello, TRACE v0.2, AAT, ACTA, receipt products) admit concrete omission or equivocation attacks. Our protocol makes omission of any side-effecting tool call detectable given one honest observer, formally verified, at ≤X µs per call, and it stops the trace-deletion attacks of 2609.30266."
- **Best-fit venue:** USENIX Security, CCS, NDSS or IEEE S&P. A systems angle (overhead, eBPF, gateways) fits NSDI/EuroSys.

### Gaps
- I did not open Sello's full paper or the Agent Flight Recorder paper to check their formal threat models beyond abstract-level text.
- The product and receipt landscape comes from an AI-generated market scan (provenant#6), and the individual products were not opened.
- TrInc/A2M-style trusted-counter precedents were not verified this session.

## Q2(c). Idea (c): Retroactive impact analysis and revocation ("blast radius" of a later-found-bad source, memory entry or tool output; provenance-based rollback)

### Takeaway
**Largely already done for agent memory** (several Jul–Sep 2026 papers compute downstream dependents of a faulty or poisoned memory and selectively repair or replay, and one proves recomputation lower bounds). **Still open:** blast radius across **external, already-committed side effects and other principals**, compensation semantics, and how *incomplete or inferred provenance* degrades blast-radius accuracy. The last point bridges ideas (a) and (c).

### Cited Findings
- *From Faulty Memories to Corrected Actions: Dependency-Guided Rollback Repair* (arXiv 2608.10502; Caili Yu et al.; Aug 2026): a typed memory-to-action graph from runtime provenance, then affected-subgraph tracing, independent-support checking, rule-guided rollback planning, and selective replay. Recovery is 85.3% vs 77.3% for the best competitor; it removes all diagnosed faulty memories and preserves all benign ones — [arXiv 2608.10502](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10502) †‡
- *MemTX: Transactional Belief Commit* (arXiv 2607.23929; Jul 2026): records carry evidence, permissions, provenance and validity. Irreversible tool calls are gated on in-flight belief state, and "**retracting a belief triggers typed cascading repair of its derived records and tool side effects**". Invariants are machine-checked over 5.5M protocol states — [arXiv 2607.23929](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23929) †
- *Forgetting Without Restarting: Execution-State Unlearning* (arXiv 2609.04875; Sep 2026): formal model in which the post-target suffix is "irreducibly tainted without token-level attribution" and exact unlearning needs ≥ T−τ+1 recomputed transitions. Provenance-guided selective replay attains that bound across prompt, compressed memory and KV cache — [arXiv 2609.04875](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04875) †
- *Revoked but Still Authoritative* (arXiv 2609.08258; Shen, Toyoda, Leung; Sep 2026): no memory system out of five enforces revocation in every case (never recorded, recorded but invisible, or visible but not enforced). Revoked policies drove unsafe actions in **43.1%** of exposed trials — [arXiv 2609.08258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08258) †‡
- More memory recovery and governance: MemTxn (durable snapshot journal; restores the complete declared active map) — [arXiv 2607.27834](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27834) †; TARL (five-action ledgers) — [arXiv 2608.03699](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03699) †; ESC-CR (taints the responsible message, then clean-room regeneration) — [arXiv 2609.26072](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26072) †; MAP-Graph (provenance-aware shared memory; revoked sources) — [arXiv 2608.10509](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10509) †; memory provenance laundering and the non-amplification firewall — [arXiv 2607.29167](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29167) †; poisoning 1.2% of LongMemEval drops accuracy 0.850→0.300 and write-time screening rejects 0/360 poisoned memories — [arXiv 2608.21230](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21230) †; MemAudit (counterfactual memory influence; attack success 70%→0% on QA) — [arXiv 2605.23723](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23723) †‡; MutMem — [arXiv 2608.02843](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02843) †; README entries MemLineage (2605.14421) and MemSecBench (2607.27080; 56.1% selective repair)
- Unlearning for agents: K-Bench — [arXiv 2609.12808](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12808) †; Trajectory Unlearning — [arXiv 2609.33639](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33639) †; OBLIVION (workflow-level skill unlearning) — [arXiv 2608.08264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08264) †; agentic tool unlearning — [arXiv 2608.21544](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21544) †
- Authority revocation: VERA (revoking edge e invalidates exactly reach(G) \ reach(G∖{e})) — [arXiv 2608.30091](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30091) †; root-scoped authorization quiescence — [arXiv 2609.21284](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21284) †
- Recovery and rollback of execution: AgentRewind (aligned context+environment checkpoints; MettleBench) — [arXiv 2608.14380](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14380) †; DART ("no criterion for whether a local restore remains semantically valid after downstream commitment") — [arXiv 2605.23311](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23311) †; *Recoverability as a System Primitive* — [arXiv 2609.13672](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13672) †; *Agentic Transaction: Towards ACID-Compliant Agent Systems* (Tsinghua DB group; KramaBench) — [arXiv 2608.13900](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13900) †, [ACID-Agent repo](https://github.com/TsinghuaDatabaseGroup/ACID-Agent) §; EvoUndo — [arXiv 2608.28363](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28363) †; *Re-derivability Decides What a Staged Agent Pipeline Recovers* — [arXiv 2609.32802](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32802) †; Atomix and ACRFence (README)
- Systems precedent: **RETRO** (OSDI 2010) records an "action history graph", undoes an unwanted action and its indirect effects by rolling back direct effects and **selectively re-executing** legitimate actions, uses predicates to limit re-execution, and applies **compensating actions for external effects** — [USENIX OSDI'10](https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi10/intrusion-recovery-using-selective-re-execution) ‡

### Inferences
- **Verdict: largely done** for in-agent memory, where 2608.10502, MemTX and 2609.04875 cover graph-based blast radius, cascading repair and a formal recomputation bound, and 2609.08258 covers the empirical revocation gap. Pitching "provenance graph → blast radius → selective rollback of memory" as new would be rejected.
- **Remaining openings:** (1) **Cross-boundary blast radius**: effects already externalized to third-party tools, other agents or organizations, where only receipts (idea b) and not internal provenance are available. Needed: compensation planning with an explicit "irreversible residue" metric; see the Irreversibility Budget (2609.00275) and Bonded Recourse (2609.01939). (2) **Blast radius under uncertain provenance**: how much contamination is missed when edges are inferred rather than observed (GRADE's grading), plus a sound over-approximation with guarantees. That is a clean (a)×(c) study. (3) **Nondeterministic re-execution**: RETRO/Warp-style selective re-execution assumes determinism; for LLM agents, re-execution needs semantic-equivalence checks, which ties to ACRFence and 2608.29381. (4) A cross-system **retroactive-revocation benchmark** spanning memory stores, KV cache and external effects.
- **Feasibility (3–6 months): moderate.** A measurement or benchmark paper is feasible; a new algorithm must clearly beat 2608.10502, MemTX and 2609.04875 on the cross-boundary setting.
- **Headline:** "With inferred dependencies (standard traces), blast-radius recall is X%; with observed edges it is Y%. Across N real deployments, revoking a poisoned source requires compensating Z external effects, of which W% are irreversible. Our compensation-aware revocation achieves …"
- **Best-fit venue:** USENIX Security/CCS for a poisoning-recovery framing; VLDB/SIGMOD for a provenance- or transaction-based rollback (RETRO lineage); NeurIPS D&B for a benchmark.

### Gaps
- I did not verify whether any of the Aug–Sep memory-rollback papers handle *external* side-effect compensation beyond MemTX's "tool side effects" phrase; their full texts were not opened.
- Warp (SOSP 2011) was not verified.

## Q2(d). Idea (d): Stale-state / TOCTOU failures (formal model, benchmark, runtime re-validation) beyond CatchBench's online stale-state board and Atomix

### Takeaway
**Largely done** for runtime re-validation mechanisms and TOCTOU attack benchmarks: a 2025 TOCTOU-Bench, an EMNLP 2026 Findings paper on browser agents, and 2026 papers on verdict freshness, decision-vs-version conflicts, dependency-scoped plan validation, race guard precision and LLM-aware concurrency control. **Still open:** *post-hoc auditability* of stale-state failures (what must be logged to detect a stale read after the fact) and an **artifact-free, naturally occurring ground-truth corpus**, which CatchBench says does not yet exist.

### Cited Findings
- *Mind the Gap* (arXiv 2508.17155, Aug 2025): TOCTOU-Bench (66 tasks); combined mitigations reduce vulnerable plans 12%→8%; tool-fusing reduces the attack window by 95% — [Awesome-LLM-Safety](https://github.com/ydyjya/Awesome-LLM-Safety) §, [staledep README](https://github.com/4ktLuffy/staledep) § (secondary)
- *Atomicity for Agents: Exposing, Exploiting, and Mitigating TOCTOU Vulnerabilities in Browser-Use Agents* (EMNLP 2026 Findings; DynWeb cases with oracle scripts) — [OSUSecLab/TOCTOU-WebAgent](https://github.com/OSUSecLab/TOCTOU-WebAgent) §
- *Approved Too Late: Verdict Staleness in LLM-Guarded Self-Adaptive Systems* (arXiv 2608.26306; Shraga, Eshel, Gorelik; Aug 2026): verdict-change rates of 5.3–48.4% at an 8-step replay shift across five environments. The Freshness-Bounded Shield estimates a validity horizon from safe-side margin and feature volatility, cutting approval expiry from 3.4–24.7% to 0–1.8%. It states a "freshness contract" — [arXiv 2608.26306](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26306) †
- *Stale Does Not Mean Unsafe* (arXiv 2609.29522; Sep 2026) separates invalidating, predicate-preserving and irrelevant races. It compares commit-time guards (global epoch, read-set version, semantic commit predicate) over 16 infrastructure tasks and 3,456 trajectories — [arXiv 2609.29522](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29522) †
- *From Version Conflicts to Decision Conflicts* (ATR; arXiv 2609.08015) re-checks only the justification conditions affected by a change, noting that "optimistic concurrency control and version checks… do not determine whether that change invalidates the pending action's justification" — [arXiv 2609.08015](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08015) †
- *Fresh Memory, Stale Plans* (PlanFence; arXiv 2609.03340): a freshness-only executor acts on the obsolete plan **in every task** of 30 live workflows; PlanFence completes all without invalid actions — [arXiv 2609.03340](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03340) †
- *When Stale Constraints Go Unchecked* (arXiv 2608.25553): under a two-record verification budget, agents inspect provenance in about 1 in 5 episodes, and superseded constraints yield stale-consistent decisions in 74.7–77.3% of episodes — [arXiv 2608.25553](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25553) †
- Concurrency control: CoAgent (Haibo Chen/Xingda Wei group; LLM judges whether a conflicting write invalidates its plan, making control "advisory") — [arXiv 2606.15376](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15376) †; *Position: MAS Should Prioritize Concurrency Control* — [arXiv 2608.18092](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18092) †; REVISE — [arXiv 2609.00643](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00643) †; SafeCommit — [arXiv 2608.04289](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04289) †; implicit stale dependencies in personalized responses — [arXiv 2608.01619](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01619) †; masking stale observations in search agents — [arXiv 2606.00408](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00408) †; SoK systematizing 39 papers including a TOCTOU category — [arXiv 2607.05743](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05743) †
- Measurement pitfalls: staledep retracted its own "TOCTOU vulnerability" prevalence claims. "Read-then-act is the definition of agency", and copying a checked literal is a snapshot, not a race. On AgentDojo, cross-tool mutation appears in 1.05% of attack-active vs 0.71% of attack-free trajectories, a length confound rather than a signal — [staledep](https://github.com/4ktLuffy/staledep) §
- CatchBench limits: the stale-state Gold injection fails the no-artifact-leakage bar, and online stale-state TPR is ≈0.06–0.16 at 5–11% FPR — [catchbench](https://github.com/yzhao062/catchbench) §. Runtime tools already exist: FreshCtx, `auditable` replay, KarmaSakshi, AuthDrift (Q1 §)

### Inferences
- **Verdict: largely done** for runtime mechanisms, TOCTOU exploitation, and "freshness contract" formalizations.
- **Remaining openings:** (1) **Post-hoc detectability**: from a log alone, can an auditor tell that an action relied on a superseded read? That requires read-version stamps or value-flow edges, which no standard telemetry has (see Q3). This ties directly to idea (a). (2) An **artifact-controlled stale-state corpus** with explicit named values (writes and reads explicit) plus a human-audited slice, exactly as CatchBench requests. (3) **Prevalence in the wild** with careful causal controls (staledep's pitfalls).
- **Feasibility: high** for (2)+(1) as a benchmark and measurement paper; low novelty for a new runtime guard.
- **Headline:** "Standard telemetry detects ≤X% of naturally occurring stale-state failures post hoc. Adding two fields (read version, validity horizon) raises it to Y%. Only Z% of CatchBench-style injected stale faults survive an artifact-leakage control."
- **Best-fit venue:** ICSE/FSE (empirical SE), NeurIPS D&B; security venues only with an exploitation angle.

### Gaps
- The full "Atomicity for Agents" paper and the TOCTOU-Bench paper were not opened; numbers for 2508.17155 come from secondary GitHub sources.

## Q2(e). Idea (e): Do richer decision-record schemas measurably improve an independent auditor's reconstruction? And privacy-preserving audits (ZK, TEE, selective disclosure)

### Takeaway
**Partially addressed.** Sufficiency and reconstructability *metrics* now exist (DEMM-Bench, Evidence Sufficiency Cards, ClaimReceipt), and several results show richer structure helps automated attribution (TraceElephant, TelemetrySuffBench, CatchBench dependency increments, Agent Flight Recorder structured queries). **No controlled study was found that renders identical runs in competing real schemas** (OTel GenAI, OpenInference, Agent-BOM, GRADE, AGT Decision BOM, IETF AAT, TRACE) and measures **independent human and/or LLM auditors'** reconstruction accuracy. Privacy-preserving audit of agents exists only in early, narrow forms (zk-MCP message format checks, TEE-isolated LLM auditors, per-action zk-SNARK guardrails); general **property audits over committed logs** remain open.

### Cited Findings
- Structure helps automated attribution: full traces raise attribution 17%→30% (TraceElephant, README); decision content and provenance are necessary — [arXiv 2608.07899](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07899) †; structured forensic queries reach 1.0 precision vs 0.013/0.077 for text search — [arXiv 2609.01931](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01931) ‡; CatchBench's POST "dependency increment" is positive across three model specifications on τ-bench but "changes sign on SWE-Gym" — [catchbench](https://github.com/yzhao062/catchbench) §
- Sufficiency and overclaiming: trace/schema presence overclaims on 75% of cases — [arXiv 2606.20634](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20634) †; sufficiency differs sharply between traces that "share a surface reading" — [arXiv 2607.12469](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12469) †
- Schema landscape: Agent-BOM (hierarchical attributed graph; README 2605.06812); GRADE; AGT Decision BOM (5-field completeness score, reconstructed from existing signals) — [AGT Tutorial 50](https://github.com/microsoft/agent-governance-toolkit/blob/main/docs/tutorials/50-decision-bom.md) §; IETF AAT draft — [datatracker](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-sharif-agent-audit-trail/) ‡; OTel GenAI (Q3) §; TRACE (Q0) §; LEDGER claim-to-evidence graphs — [arXiv 2608.18398](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18398) †; DNative-Twin reconstructable decisions — [arXiv 2609.03787](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03787) †
- Non-identifiability of responsibility from logs: delegation-scoped execution "is not identifiable from standard observables" — [arXiv 2606.09692](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09692) †. Auditor independence grading (principal, substrate, evidence) — [arXiv 2609.18272](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18272) †
- Human auditing: the digest search for human-auditor or user-study papers on agent traces found only a visualization tool (TraceView, arXiv 2606.22110) †, and no controlled schema-comparison study — [arXiv 2606.22110](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22110)
- Privacy-preserving audit:
  - zk-MCP pairs Circom ZK proofs with MCP to verify message format and type and support "mutual audits" without revealing content — [arXiv 2512.14737](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.14737) †
  - Agentic Witnessing isolates an LLM auditor in a TEE that answers Boolean queries over private data with a signed hash chain binding the reasoning trace to the dataset and the TEE root. Demonstrated on 21 papers' codebases — [arXiv 2604.24203](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24203) †
  - NiyamAI: per-action zk-SNARK, ~2.26 s proving and ~53 ms verification — [arXiv 2608.07167](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07167) †
  - Cryptographic certificates of validity (policy predicate → succinct proof) — [arXiv 2606.23768](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23768) †
  - Receipts vs ZK cost: <15 ms HMAC receipts vs ~180 s for zkLLM — [arXiv 2603.10060](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10060) †
  - TRACE: TEE attestation plus SCITT anchoring — [trace-spec](https://github.com/agentrust-io/trace-spec) §
  - Sello: HPKE-encrypted receipts readable only by the owner — [arXiv 2606.04193](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04193) †
  - OTel #402 proposes `gen_ai.tool.args_hash`/`result_hash` so "an auditor [can] verify a known input/output pair without exposing the payload" — [semantic-conventions-genai#402](https://github.com/open-telemetry/semantic-conventions-genai/issues/402) §
- A digest search for "selective disclosure" in agent-audit abstracts returned no hits (Jun–Sep 2026) † (negative result from the local corpus).

### Inferences
- **Verdict: partially addressed.** Metrics exist, but no **causal evidence that schema choice changes auditor outcomes**, which is exactly what a standards body or regulator would want. That makes a controlled "same runs, many schemas, independent auditors" study novel and policy-relevant. LLM auditors are a scalable proxy; a small expert human panel adds credibility.
- **Privacy-preserving line:** a concrete open problem is proving **log properties** ("every payment above €X had an approval receipt", "no tool call touched data class Y", "no omitted receipts in window W") over a Merkle-committed agent log, without revealing it, via ZK or a TEE-hosted verifier. Compare cost with TRACE-style attestation and with receipts.
- **Feasibility:** the LLM-auditor study is high (3 months, reusing CatchBench/GRADE/TelemetrySuffBench runs rendered in 5–6 schemas); the human study is medium (IRB, recruiting); the ZK property-audit prototype is medium-hard (circuits over hash-chained logs; start with a TEE).
- **Headline:** "On identical runs, auditors answer K audit questions with A% accuracy from OTel spans vs B% from dependency-augmented records. Decision→provenance links explain most of the gain. A commitment-only (privacy-preserving) rendering retains C% of that accuracy."
- **Best-fit venue:** FAccT or CHI (human auditors, accountability); USENIX Security, CCS or PETS (privacy-preserving audit); NeurIPS D&B (benchmark); ICSE/FSE (schema engineering).

- The Agent-BOM paper's evaluation details and any follow-ups citing it could not be checked (Semantic Scholar blocked).

## Q3. What is the status of the OpenTelemetry GenAI agent semantic conventions (agent spans, tool spans), and are there proposals to add decision or dependency information?

### Takeaway
The GenAI conventions now live in their own repository, and **everything is still "Development"** (no release; the schema URL is "TODO"). Agent-level spans are `create_agent`, `invoke_agent` (client and internal), `invoke_workflow`, `plan` and `execute_tool`, plus memory operations. Tool arguments and results are **Opt-In**. **No accepted convention records data dependencies or decision rationale.** Several open proposals (Jun–Sep 2026) would add decision-point IDs and outcomes, agent type fingerprints and parent prompts, run/step IDs, side-effect classes, argument/result hashes, attestation references and causal span links.

### Inferences
- The only dependency-like structure OTel provides is the **parent–child span tree plus trace context**, i.e., the execution layer, not *what data a step relied on*. #309 (causal span links) and #402/#239 (decision IDs, hashes) are the closest to a dependency or decision layer, and none is merged. That matches TelemetrySuffBench's finding that OTel-compatible views keep detection but lose origin localization.
- A research contribution that proposes and **empirically justifies** a minimal "dependency/decision" extension (e.g., `gen_ai.step.reads` = set of span/resource IDs with versions, `gen_ai.decision.basis` = hash or ref) could feed directly into these issues. That gives idea (a) or (e) standards impact.

## Q4. What concrete technical questions do EU AI Act Article 12 (record-keeping), Article 19 / 26(6) (log retention), and the 2026 Digital Omnibus timeline raise for agent logging?

### Takeaway
Article 12 mandates a *capability* for automatic event logging over the system's lifetime, oriented to three purposes: risk and substantial-modification identification, post-market monitoring, and deployer monitoring. It sets **no minimum field set except for remote biometric identification** and does not mandate tamper-evidence. Retention of at least six months applies only to logs "under their control" and yields to data-protection law. The Digital Omnibus (Reg. (EU) 2026/1744) deferred high-risk obligations to **2 Dec 2027** (Annex III) and **2 Aug 2028** (Annex I). Meanwhile ISO/IEC 24970 and prEN 18229-1 (the "logging" harmonized standards) are in final stages and are converging on hash-chained records with recording independence. That leaves a 12–24-month window in which research on *sufficiency* and *completeness* can shape what "compliant agent logs" mean in practice.

### Cited Findings
- Art. 12(1): "High-risk AI systems shall technically allow for the automatic recording of events (logs) over the lifetime of the system." Art. 12(2): logging must enable recording events relevant for "(a) identifying situations that may result in the high-risk AI system presenting a risk within the meaning of Article 79(1) or in a substantial modification; (b) facilitating the post-market monitoring referred to in Article 72; and (c) monitoring the operation… referred to in Article 26(5)." Art. 12(3) specifies minimum fields (period of each use, reference database, input data that led to a match, identity of verifying persons) **only for Annex III point 1(a)** remote biometric systems — [GitHub reproduction of OJ text (lawve-ai)](https://github.com/lawve-ai/awesome-legal-skills/blob/main/skills/eu-ai-act-knowledge-base-oliver-schmidt-prietz/references/core/regulation-title-III-high-risk.md), [camlsys/aireg-bench articles.txt](https://github.com/camlsys/aireg-bench/blob/main/articles.txt) §; official: [EUR-Lex 2024/1689](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj)
- Art. 19(1) (providers) and Art. 26(6) (deployers): keep automatically generated logs "**to the extent such logs are under their control**… of at least six months, **unless provided otherwise in applicable Union or national law, in particular in Union law on the protection of personal data**". Financial institutions keep logs within their financial-services documentation. Art. 19(2) and Art. 21 give competent authorities access to logs "under the control" of the provider — [GitHub reproduction (lawve-ai)](https://github.com/lawve-ai/awesome-legal-skills/blob/main/skills/eu-ai-act-knowledge-base-oliver-schmidt-prietz/references/core/regulation-title-III-high-risk.md) §
- Omnibus timeline: Reg. (EU) 2026/1744, in force 27 July 2026, moved high-risk application to 2 Dec 2027 (Annex III) and 2 Aug 2028 (AI in regulated products) — README of awesome-auditable-ai; corroborated by TRACE spec §1 ("Article 113, third paragraph, point (c), as replaced by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744… 2 December 2027… 2 August 2028") — [trace-v0.2 spec](https://github.com/agentrust-io/trace-spec/blob/main/spec/trace-v0.2.md) §
- A claimed calendar gap: "Article 86's right to an explanation has applied since 2 August 2026, while the Article 12 logging and Annex IV documentation that could evidence one were deferred to 2 December 2027" — [arXiv 2609.06445](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06445) † (the paper's legal reading; not independently verified).
- Tamper-evidence is not legally required: "Article 12 of the EU AI Act requires a logging capability; it does not prescribe a TRACE level or tamper-evident format" — [TRACE LIMITATIONS](https://github.com/agentrust-io/trace-spec/blob/main/LIMITATIONS.md) §
- Standards convergence: ISO/IEC 24970 (AI system logging) is in final ballot (2026-08-28; joint JTC 1/SC 42 and CEN-CENELEC JTC 21). prEN 18229-1 (AI Trustworthiness Framework, Part 1: Logging) closed public hearing 2026-08-20. The IETF AAT draft was updated 2026-09-05. All three converge on "hash-chained, timestamped, tamper-evident records; pre-execution capture of denials and escalations; a recording-independence gradient…; optional external timestamp anchoring; and… metadata for decision reproducibility". The same post notes that EU AI Act Art. 12/19/26(6), China TC260 agent guidelines, Singapore's agentic framework, SOC 2, PCI DSS v4.0.1 and ISO/IEC 42001 share a logging theme — [aaif/wg-governance-risk-and-regulatory#9](https://github.com/aaif/wg-governance-risk-and-regulatory/issues/9) § (community post; dates not independently verified)
- Outdated claims circulate: the AAT draft (as summarized) and OTel #307 describe Article 12 obligations as effective or enforced "August 2026" — [datatracker](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-sharif-agent-audit-trail/) ‡, [OTel #307](https://github.com/open-telemetry/semantic-conventions-genai/issues) §. This conflicts with the Omnibus deferral above.

### Inferences
Technical research questions raised for agents:
1. **What is an "event" for an agent?** Model calls, tool calls, memory writes, delegations, plan changes. Art. 12(2)(a) requires logs that let one *identify risk situations*, which is an attributability requirement (idea a), not just a volume requirement.
2. **Substantial modification detection** (Art. 12(2)(a)): prompts, harness, tools and models change continuously. Logging a content-hash identity of the agent type (cf. OTel #334's `agent.type.id`) is one answer; the question is what granularity separates a modification from ordinary configuration drift.
3. **"Under their control"** (Art. 19/26(6)): in agent systems, much of the causal chain happens at third-party MCP servers or A2A agents outside the provider's or deployer's control. Receiver-side receipts (idea b) are the technical means to bring cross-party evidence into scope. Who is obliged to keep them?
4. **Data protection vs completeness**: retention yields to GDPR, while tamper-evident append-only logs resist erasure. The AAT draft proposes hashing plus tombstones; **redactable-yet-verifiable agent logs** are an open design problem (the digest search found no agent-specific paper).
5. **No minimum field set** outside biometrics: harmonized standards (ISO/IEC 24970, prEN 18229-1) will fill it. Empirical sufficiency results (TelemetrySuffBench, DEMM-Bench, idea a/e) could inform whether "converging shape" logs are actually sufficient for attribution.
6. **Deployer monitoring** (Art. 12(2)(c) → 26(5)) calls for streaming, LIVE-state audit signals (CatchBench LIVE) under a false-alarm budget.
7. **Independence**: no rule forbids the agent from being its own recorder, but 2609.30266 shows self-recorded traces can be deleted. Standards' "recording-independence gradient" needs a testable definition.

### Gaps
- The official OJ text and Omnibus text were not opened directly (EUR-Lex blocked). Article texts come from GitHub reproductions.
- The Art. 86 applicability claim is the paper's interpretation.
- The ISO/IEC 24970 and prEN 18229-1 contents (field lists) were not accessible.

## Q5. Additional promising gaps found

### Takeaway
Besides ideas (a)–(e), five gaps look under-served and newcomer-friendly: **(1)** tamper-proof trace capture *outside* the agent for local coding harnesses; **(2)** adversarial robustness of *post-hoc* auditors and attributors to crafted trace content; **(3)** accountability and verification without determinism (record/replay at the model boundary); **(4)** redactable-yet-verifiable agent logs (GDPR vs append-only); **(5)** empirical tests of whether "converging standard" log shapes are sufficient for attribution.

### Cited Findings
- (1) Agents can delete their traces in all tested harnesses but one, and the authors call for "independent interception" — [arXiv 2609.30266](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30266) †. Building blocks: eBPF observability (AgentSight, PACMI 2025) — [eunomia.dev](https://github.com/eunomia-bpf/eunomia.dev) §; kernel-evidence value — [arXiv 2609.28915](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28915) †; ActPlane (README)
- (2) Monitors can be evaded through the content they read: plan injection achieves 25–33% CoT-monitor evasion with "clean traces" — [arXiv 2609.15989](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15989) †; instrumental monitor evasion emerges under ordinary task pressure — [arXiv 2609.30217](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30217) †; memory attacks look like model failure (the "Misattribution Gap") — [arXiv 2605.22842](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22842) †. README entries: SLEIGHT-Bench and "Adaptive Attacks on Trusted Monitors" cover *online* monitors. I found no paper attacking **post-hoc failure-attribution or audit LLMs** (e.g., AgenTracer, CatchBench judges) with adversarially crafted tool outputs designed to shift blame (negative result from the digest search).
- (3) PeerReview-style accountability assumes deterministic nodes — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1323293.1294279) ‡; "replay preconditions are unmet in every scored trace" — [arXiv 2607.12469](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12469) †; TRACE formalizes `reproduced`/`diverged`/`not-attempted` re-execution outcomes over a content-addressed `input_closure` — [trace-v0.2 spec](https://github.com/agentrust-io/trace-spec/blob/main/spec/trace-v0.2.md) §; transport-level deterministic replay tool — [arXiv 2607.16200](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16200) †
- (4) Retention yields to data-protection law (Art. 19/26(6)) — [GitHub OJ reproduction](https://github.com/lawve-ai/awesome-legal-skills/blob/main/skills/eu-ai-act-knowledge-base-oliver-schmidt-prietz/references/core/regulation-title-III-high-risk.md) §; the AAT draft relies on hashing plus tombstones — [datatracker](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-sharif-agent-audit-trail/) ‡; the digest search for GDPR, erasure or redactable logs in agent-audit abstracts found no dedicated paper (negative result) †
- (5) Standards converge on a record *shape* (hash-chained, timestamped, denials captured) — [aaif#9](https://github.com/aaif/wg-governance-risk-and-regulatory/issues/9) §, but TelemetrySuffBench shows shape-level telemetry loses origin localization — [arXiv 2608.07899](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07899) †
- Related open problem lists: the survey's challenges (unified trace schemas, claim-level/semantic provenance, provenance-aware safety, realistic execution-trace benchmarks, recovery-oriented evaluation, privacy-aware audit infrastructure) — [arXiv 2606.04990](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04990) †; README "Open gaps" ("Standard decision records remain open… no widely adopted cross-vendor schema captures decisions, dependencies, rationale, integrity, and responsibility together"; "Recovery evaluation is emerging… no agreed cross-system recovery protocol or metric suite") — [README](https://github.com/yzhao062/awesome-auditable-ai)

### Inferences
- **Ranking for a newcomer** (novelty × feasibility × venue fit), my judgment:
  1. **(a) minimal-sufficient/attributable logging**, optionally with a stale-read post-hoc detectability sub-study (d). Open core, rich public data, and three venue families.
  2. **(b) omission-resistant receipts + outside-the-agent capture**, merging (b) with gap (1). Clear threat model and top security venues, but fast-moving competition.
  3. **(e) controlled auditor study across schemas.** Novel evidence type with standards and policy impact.
  4. **Gap (2): adversarial attacks on post-hoc attributors/auditors.** Likely novel and security-venue-friendly, but check overlap with the failure-attribution literature.
  5. **(c) and (d)** only in their narrowed forms (cross-boundary blast radius under uncertain provenance; an artifact-free stale-state corpus).
- A **combination** that avoids crowded ground: "What must an agent log so that failures stay attributable *and* revocable?" Formalize attributability, derive minimal fields (dependency edges + read versions + decision-content hash), show that blast-radius recall and stale-read detection both depend on them, and propose them as an OTel extension (#309/#402). This connects (a), (c), (d) and Q3.

### Gaps
- None of the "no paper found" statements is exhaustive. They rely on a ~22.4k-abstract local corpus (cs.AI-centric categories, 2026-05-25 → 2026-09-29) plus GitHub search. cs.CR-, cs.SE- and cs.DB-only papers and venue proceedings (USENIX Security '26, CCS '26, VLDB '26) could not be scanned directly.
